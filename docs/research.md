# Research and provenance

nachisto adapts [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) v4.0.0 for Russian. It keeps the upstream A–F category framework and adapts its workflow, then adds Russian-specific rules and category G. Patterns and examples were rewritten for Russian-language model output.

The sources below motivate conservative editing rules. They do not certify nachisto's quality, prove text provenance, or guarantee equal performance across models and genres.

## Primary references

- [blader/humanizer v4.0.0](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) — basis of the Russian adaptation.
- [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) — structural editorial guidance, not an academic detector.
- [Википедия: Признаки сгенерированности текста](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Википедия:Признаки_сгенерированности_текста) — Russian-language structural guidance.
- [smixs/humanizer-ru](https://github.com/smixs/humanizer-ru) and [ilyautov/humanizer-ru](https://github.com/ilyautov/humanizer-ru) — related projects and case discussions.

| [ITMO report on a 5,500-text Russian-language study](https://news.itmo.ru/ru/science/it/news/14495/) — 2025 | Report on 5,500 Russian-language materials | Reported 94% human/AI and about 80% with a third “paraphrased AI” category; a result of one system and corpus |
| [Linguistic Characteristics of AI-Generated Text: A Survey, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05136) | Field survey | Background for conservative pattern design, not a quality certificate |

## Evidence limits

- Many Russian-language studies use small or homogeneous samples.
- Perceived AI-likeness is a property of reader perception, not proof of provenance.
- A single marker does not prove that a text was generated by AI.
- The sources motivate editing heuristics; they do not validate nachisto across every genre, model, or host.
- Removing a pattern is an editorial decision, not a detector score or a guarantee of “human” writing.

The project's goal is readable prose with preserved meaning. It is not an AI-detector bypass tool.

## Documentation conventions

The repository documentation follows these public guides:

- [GitHub: About the repository README file](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-readmes) — explain what the project does, why it is useful, how to start, and where to get help; keep longer material outside the root README.

- [Google developer documentation style guide: Highlights](https://developers.google.com/style/highlights) — direct language, active voice, second person, sentence-case headings, descriptive links, and code formatting.
- [Make a README](https://www.makeareadme.com/) — practical sections for description, installation, usage, support, contributing, and license.

The root README is the entry point. Detailed installation, compatibility, research, contribution, security, and test material lives in linked documents so the first screen stays focused.
